What is Labor Day?

Labor Day is a U.S. federal holiday observed on the first Monday in September to honor the social and economic contributions of American workers. In 2026, Labor Day falls on Monday, September 7.

Last updated: 7 September 2026

Why is Labor Day celebrated?

The holiday grew out of the labor movement in the late nineteenth century. According to the U.S. Department of Labor, labor activists pushed for a national day recognizing workers' contributions to the country's strength, prosperity and well-being.

When did Labor Day become a federal holiday?

The first Labor Day celebration was held in New York City on September 5, 1882. States began adopting the holiday in the following years, and Congress made the first Monday in September a legal holiday in 1894.
